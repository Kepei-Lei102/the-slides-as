Watch one gate become three

① NOT — tie the inputs together
A Ā
NAND(A, A) = NOT A — because A·A is just A, then the bubble flips it.
A=0→1 · A=1→0 ✓
1 NAND = 1 NOT. (Turing Complete's opening level — some of you have done this with your own hands.)
② AND — flip the flip
A B A·B
A NAND is an AND that lies. So make it lie twice: NAND, then the NOT trick on its output.
2 NANDs = 1 AND.
③ OR — De Morgan's re-costume
A B A+B
Flip both inputs, then NAND them: "NOT(Ā·B̄)" — and De Morgan says that IS "A OR B". Check inputs 00, 01, 10, 11: the result is 0111, just like OR.
3 NANDs = 1 OR.
Logical sufficiency: these constructions provide NOT, AND and OR from NAND. That proves what can be built, not what every chip contains. Real designs choose from many cell functions.

The other universal gate — and the Moon

🌗 NOR reached the Moon.
The Apollo Guidance Computer used dual three-input NOR devices for its logic. That is a documented hardware choice, not a rule for all computers.

Try the mirrored construction: NOR(A,A) gives NOT A. Inverting NOR(A,B) gives OR.
Can NOR also make AND?
First form NOT A and NOT B using tied-input NOR gates. Feed them to another NOR.

Check all four rows: NOT((NOT A) OR (NOT B)) gives 0001. Three NORs have become AND.
Keep the distinction: a universal gate can build every Boolean function; a real computer also needs memory, timing, power and interfaces. The historical three-input NOR is beyond these boards’ gate-drawing scope.

🤖 What a machine does with it
Turn every attribute into a Boolean variable ("house 2 is blue" = one bit). Every clue becomes a constraint on those bits. Solving = finding an assignment where every clue outputs 1 — working backward from a table, which is related to the backward reasoning in this lesson… at industrial scale.
From puzzle to verification: express a design’s requirements as constraints. A solver can search for an assignment that violates a property: a counterexample. The model and the property must describe the intended system.

Working backward: table → circuit

Someone hands you only a fingerprint and demands the circuit:

ABF
001
010
100
111
The recipe:
① Find every row where the output is 1.
② Describe each such row with ANDs — put a NOT on every input showing 0.
③ OR the row-descriptions together. Done.
Row 0 0 → 1:  (NOT A) AND (NOT B)
Row 1 1 → 1:  A AND B

F = ((NOT A) AND (NOT B)) OR (A AND B)
— "both off, or both on": you just built an equality detector, XOR's twin.
For any finite Boolean table, a circuit exists. If every row is 0, a constant-zero function such as A AND NOT A handles that case. This claim concerns combinational Boolean functions, not arbitrary physical behaviour.
Match positions, not just the count of 1s. XOR is 0110; this table is 1001. A table such as 0011 also has two 1s but simply outputs A. If no listed gate fits, use the row-building recipe.

The pro's version

The row-by-row construction can be long. The alarm has five output-1 rows. Each three-signal AND term needs two two-input AND gates; combining five terms needs four two-input OR gates, plus input inversion.
There is an algebra that dismantles the bloat.
One glimpse: (W·M·P) OR (W·M·NOT P) — two terms differing in exactly one letter — merge into W·M, and the letter vanishes. Rule by rule, the longer construction can be replaced by two gates. There's even a visual tool that does it by eye.
Optional A2 preview: algebra and Karnaugh maps can reduce a construction’s gate count. Physical cost also depends on the cells and wiring. In AS/IG questions, follow the supplied structure; this is not an extra simplification requirement.

Two gates just did arithmetic

A B XOR AND Sum Carry

the machine you just forged — it has a name: the half adder

The plant pays. Last week, a hole ended with: "1⊕1 = 0… which is what you write for 1+1. Hold that thought." You held it for a week. XOR writes the sum. AND raises the carry. Binary addition — the thing from U2 — is these two gates.
And it scales. Give it one upgrade (a carry-in, so columns can chain) and copies of this circuit add numbers of any width. Your calculator, your phone, the machine showing this slide — adding by pure logic, billions of times a second.
The week's arc closes: two signal ranges made binary convenient (U3a) → two symbols made gates → gates make arithmetic. A computer doesn't do maths despite being made of logic. Arithmetic IS logic.

Everything you built today… forgets

The combinational circuits we traced respond to the current inputs after propagation delays. Their Boolean tables do not describe stored state.

But your machine remembers. Your save file. Your high score. This very slide, sitting in RAM.

A single memoryless gate is not a storage element. Connecting gates with feedback can create state. What changes when outputs can affect future inputs?
